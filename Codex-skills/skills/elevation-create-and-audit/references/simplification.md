# Evidence-owned simplification

## Source location and inclusion contract

Keep a durable atlas of source path, SHA-256, page/detail title, region coordinates, rotation, coordinate conventions, native primitive/text IDs, and view-specific calibration. Plan and elevation scales may differ. Bind source observations to this atlas; a changed source invalidates affected evidence. Keep viewpoint correspondence internally even when reference bubbles are excluded from customer output. Earlier generated drawings locate possible defects; their geometry, labels, and symbols are not replacement source evidence.

Before rendering, record each feature's owner, semantic category, evidence, retain/simplify/omit decision, and reason. Source visibility alone does not make a feature relevant. Use the agreed main assembly plus context that bounds or dimensions it. Keep source observations separate from solved physical edges and annotation layout. Do not identify owners solely by coordinates, material, or primitive shape.

## Simplify appearance without destroying construction

- Preserve recognizable physical silhouettes, including curves and attached coordination outlines, thin members, seams, closing edges, steps, and intentional gaps. Simplifying appliance interiors does not permit deleting its supported envelope.
- A genuine contour may cross the extraction boundary. Clip its source interval to the agreed scope instead of dropping the entire primitive because one endpoint is outside. Do not substitute coincident grid dashes or invent a wall from a reference line.
- When a fixture is intentionally omitted, repair only corroborated, collinear intervals of the same physical edge interrupted by that fixture's source mask. Preserve real openings, owner changes, and foreground occlusion. A faint lamp is not evidence of a notch; removing one is not permission to bridge every gap.
- Keep material regions and assembly bounds non-stroking unless independently evidenced as physical edges. A continuous physical band has one assembly identity across material regions; material partitions cannot create an internal seam. Enumerate all coincident contributors before trimming a false crossing stroke.
- For a physical object or context outline selected for customer output, solid line style is not sufficient: verify continuous visible coverage between its source-bound endpoints and intended corners. Repair only source-supported collinear breaks; keep registered genuine gaps, openings, slat spaces, and separate operation/reference marks blank or dashed as appropriate. Do not let dimension-label backgrounds hide the outline.
- Keep descriptions with their actual owners. If an unnecessary callout is omitted, remove its leader, arrow and terminator circle/dot together. A physical knob, burner or other circular object must not be deleted merely because it has no text. Move or remove a redundant label rather than masking a physical boundary with its background.
- For simplified drawings requested with clean solid outlines, omit generic non-dimensional source/reference marks by default. Explicit source-verified exception to the earlier omission lesson: preserve regular door/drawer `opening_operation` marks as a separate owner-bound annotation layer unless the user explicitly excludes them. Do not convert them to solid physical geometry or numeric dimensions. Required view-reference arrows remain a separate allowed layer. Preserve excluded source evidence in audit registers.
- A non-cabinet fixture or light may be omitted only when it is decorative, undimensioned and outside coordination scope. If its outline, position or spacing is dimensioned or provides a required witness, retain a simplified source-bound silhouette and its complete dimensions. Keep any unmeasured physical size explicitly provisional and do not turn the fixture into cabinet geometry.

## Dimension recovery and clearance

Inventory unlabeled witnesses independently of recognized text. Resolve necessary intervals by exact confirmed-chain arithmetic, matched cross-view endpoints, then calibrated source measurement. Record endpoints, formula/method, uncertainty, and justified precision. Noisy OCR cross-checks native text and enlarged witnesses; it does not automatically override them. Conflicts remain explicit.

Route native and newly calculated/estimated dimensions through the same annotation-clearance solver. Keep true structural bindings distinct from visible extension starts. For surrounding dimensions, measure clearance beyond the complete visible object/context envelope, including an outer wall between the bound point and the dimension lane. Never move the structural endpoint just to obtain a visual gap.

## Owner's simplified factory-review profile

For this owner's simplified plan/elevation sets, unless explicitly overridden:

1. Physical geometry: solid black relevant object/context outlines.
2. Dimensions: complete blue graphics; confirmed numeric values without yellow, necessary resolved calculated/estimated values on pale yellow, no visible `INF`.
3. Opening marks: source-supported, owner-bound door/drawer marks with their original pattern in elevations; retain verified 2D plan swing curves when requested. A 2D plan exception is not evidence of reliable 3D plan direction. Preserve unresolved spatial axes rather than inventing depth for a future viewer.
4. Important descriptions: concise black names, material codes and necessary construction notes.

For plans delivered with corresponding elevations, retain compact, source-linked view-reference labels by default unless the user explicitly requests their omission. Preserve verified detail/sheet identifiers and viewing directions; keep the labels readable, secondary to dimensions, and clear of object outlines and other annotations. Reduce marker size rather than moving physical geometry or structural dimension endpoints. Keep view labels in the reference-annotation layer, never physical geometry or door/drawer operations. If correspondence is unresolved, record the issue rather than invent a reference. A standalone plan without corresponding elevations does not automatically require view labels.

Omit decorative textures, floor patterns, grids, unrelated reference bubbles, room/entry fragments, redundant material leaders and small undimensioned lighting symbols not needed for cabinet coordination. A registered, labeled view-reference circle is not an orphan callout. Do not suppress a dimensioned physical band/recess or fixture merely because it relates to lighting. A necessary external note may retain a correctly connected leader. This profile controls presentation, not source truth or a universal architectural dashed-line convention. Future project instructions override it.

## Verification boundary

Check both required content present and excluded content absent against an independently prepared source inventory. Inspect actual SVG/PDF geometry plus PNG and rasterized PDF, at whole-view and detail scales. Mutate exports while leaving the correct model unchanged: add an unregistered bubble/orphan leader/texture/grid; omit, duplicate, mislabel or reverse a registered view reference; dash a wall; delete a recognizable curve or repaired board edge; bridge a genuine opening; remove an unlabeled interval's value; reverse its inference styling; extend an inferred witness through the outer wall. The normal validator must detect applicable defects, alongside existing dimension, symbol, band-continuity, thin-edge, gap, and view tests. Permit only evidence-bound registered view labels, not arbitrary circles or triangles.

Keep source fidelity, model consistency, export correctness, layout, visual review, repeatability and user acceptance separate and bound to current artifact hashes. Repeated builds prove repeatability, not accuracy. A skill snapshot can preserve a verified workflow lesson while drawing acceptance and project measurements remain pending. Do not promote project coordinates, component counts, estimates, or unresolved interpretations into shared defaults.
