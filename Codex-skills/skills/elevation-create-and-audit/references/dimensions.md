# Dimensions and inference

## Component size versus relationship measurements

Classify each retained source dimension as component-size or relationship data, or record an explicit exclusion. Relationships include object spacing, clearances, offsets and heights from structural datums. Store stable subjects, two structural anchors, source views, evidence and measurement-specific uncertainty; group subjects may make the same relationship available through several children without duplicating it. Recompute endpoint differences from the current model while preserving original source observations. Do not replace an unrepresented placement dimension with an object's width, depth or height, or treat the placement as confirmed merely because the object exists.

For coordinated 3D delivery, carry these bindings into the shared scene and audit actual graphics in 3D, checking SVG, portable HTML and fallback. Confirmed center positions do not inherit uncertainty solely from an illustrative fixture diameter. Necessary uncertain endpoints or controlling measurements do require pale-yellow numeric styling. Keep customer identifiers and values project-local and use the 3D companion for selection and camera behavior.

## Thin components and conflicting chains

A newly recognized thickness needs its own source region and traced witness endpoints. Do not reuse endpoints from another observation merely because its number matches. Represent dimensioned thin members as physical components, including their top, underside, exposed ends, and interrupted spans.

Preserve appliance coordination edges that terminate adjoining construction. Solve horizontal opening width separately from appliance height; countertop height alone does not establish the appliance top or bottom. Missing height evidence stays unresolved rather than being inferred by alignment alone.

For a conflict, retain the original observation, bound endpoints, exact solved difference, residual, evidence, and resolution status. A test may pass because it detects the conflict; the dimension itself has not passed. Block drawing approval while a required chain remains unresolved. Do not hide a residual in invented trim or relabel a source dimension to force agreement. Document allowed area changes when adding previously omitted construction.


Keep recognized observations separate from object assignment. Retain original text, source region, witness lines, endpoint ticks, and exact start/end boundary references. Solve with fractions or equivalent exact unit arithmetic. Verify each value equals its bound datum difference and ordered horizontal/vertical chains reconcile.

An unlabeled dimension witness is still evidence that the referenced outline or datum matters, but it supplies no numeric value by itself. Preserve a visible physical boundary supported by that witness, classify the mark separately from geometry, and keep the measurement unresolved unless a confirmed chain, calibrated source geometry, or a coordinated view supplies the value. Do not omit the boundary merely because OCR found no number.

A coordinated view may resolve an unlabeled interval only after the two views' orientation, assembly identity, and structural endpoints have been matched. Calculate the residual with exact arithmetic, keep its sign and physical direction separate, and bind the positive physical length to the short interval actually shown by the witnesses. Recompute the affected chain and rebind following endpoints while preserving confirmed downstream spans. If the correspondence or chain still conflicts, retain it as pending rather than forcing closure.

Distinguish overall top, cabinet top, face top, rails, closeouts, floor/plinth datums, door splits, and internal details. A recognized number with incorrect endpoints is still an error. Explicitly document any observations excluded from customer output; never automatically classify an unrendered record as internal.

Inference order: exact residual from confirmed chains; supported repetition/symmetry within an established assembly; alignment to adjoining confirmed contours; image proportion last. Record method, formula, alternatives, confidence, and source region. Resolve contradictory constraints visibly in the audit instead of silently adjusting geometry. Choose rounding for project units; nearest 1/16 inch was an example convention only.

Assign stable IDs to dimensions and components. Share records between annotations and schedules. Each displayed measurement must have a dimension record, two structural endpoint references, a dimension line, witness or extension lines, ticks, and a label. Internal observations and redundant derived values may remain in schedules or audit data with an explicit display policy; they do not become customer annotations merely because arithmetic produced a value. Each rendered dimension must have a record.

Use explicit inference status for background styling, independent of display text. Display a necessary, resolved inferred dimension as its numeric value and units on pale yellow with the same complete dimension graphics as a confirmed dimension. Do not append `INF`, `TBC`, or explanatory words to customer-facing dimension text. Keep unresolved residuals internal and block source approval when they affect required geometry.

Measure formatted values using the rendering font at intended output size. Prefer centered labels in genuine dimension-line gaps. Rotate text and background together. If text plus clearance does not fit, or conflicts remain, use the nearest clear lane and an angled leader with a short horizontal end. Stagger neighboring labels deterministically. Move the complete dimension chain into a blank appliance opening if appropriate, not merely its labels. Preserve exact endpoints while routing visible lines clear of surrounding geometry.

Store resolved label bounds, rotation, ticks, line gaps, leader points, true structural references, drawn extension starts/ends, and verified readability. Keep the true measurement endpoint distinct from the visible extension-line start. If a wall, return, or other outline lies between them, retain the structural binding while routing the drawn extension from outside the complete visible envelope. Preserve visible object-to-extension gaps and overshoots, measured from that envelope rather than only from the bound endpoint. Move the dimension line outward if needed. The historical 10-pixel gap/overshoot at 2400×1800 is a scalable default; adapt to output size and print readability.

Apply this same clearance procedure to source-native and newly inferred dimensions; a later-added yellow value must not bypass it. For a calibrated estimate, record uncertainty and avoid precision finer than the evidence supports.
