# Plans, elevations, sections, and symbols

## Read the drawing set as a system

Use plan, elevation, section, detail, schedule, legend, and note relationships. A line may represent a physical edge in one view and an operation or projection in another. Confirm owner face, view direction, cutting plane, scale, and nearby references before assigning meaning.

Classify visible marks before interpretation:

- physical visible edge or confirmed seam;
- hidden, overhead, or beyond line;
- centreline, grid, datum, or alignment line;
- cut, section, break, or reference line;
- opening, operation, hinge, access, or movement mark;
- material hatch, grain, surface texture, or directional pattern;
- dimension, extension, witness, leader, or terminator;
- note, tag, revision, elevation, section, or detail callout;
- unresolved source symbol.

Preserve a symbol's observable appearance separately: path or shape, solid/dashed pattern, line weight, orientation, endpoints, owner face, clipping, and repetition. Record interpretation separately.

## X, V, W, diamond, and diagonal marks

These marks often communicate operation, opening direction, hinge/handle orientation, access, or an overlapping pair of operations in cabinet and door software. They are not universal. The same appearance can have different meanings between firms, projects, objects, or software settings.

Do not create a physical divider, paired doors, bracing, void, glazing, or a new material area from one of these marks alone. A confirmed physical seam remains geometry independent of the mark. If the appearance is clear but meaning is not, preserve the mark when needed and classify the meaning as unresolved.

Material does not determine operation marks. Wood, glass, mirror, metal, painted, or other faces may share the same operation symbol. Conversely, a fixed wood rail, return, closeout, toe kick, or panel should not receive a mark merely because it is wood.

## Simplification

Retain geometry, material boundaries, operation cues, view references, and necessary dimensions. Simplify dense grain, hatch, or texture only when the result remains recognizable and does not alter material scope or direction. Match the customer's symbol family, orientation, and solid/dashed appearance where legible; adjust spacing and line weight only for output clarity.

Use ISO and PSPC sources as general or project-specific references only according to applicability. PSPC standards govern PSPC work when adopted; they are not automatically the house style for private Vancouver interiors.
