# Material-system routing

Material notes identify finish or product scope; they do not automatically define every boundary, substrate, attachment, or operation. Corroborate material tags with leaders, hatches, schedules, details, and adjacent views.

## Wood and composite wood

- Use the project-specified NAAWS edition for architectural woodwork requirements. AWMAC identifies NAAWS as its current North American architectural woodwork standard and provides tracked errata.
- Use AWI 100 as useful submittal guidance when specified or adopted; its public requirements describe plan, elevation, section, details, dimensions, grain direction, interfaces, and attachment information.
- For Canadian composite wood products, check current CANFER scope, certification, labelling, and record-keeping requirements. Do not infer compliance from a WD material code or drawing hatch.

## Stone, tile, and terrazzo

- Use TTMAC installation/specification guides when specified for Canadian tile, terrazzo, or dimensional stone work.
- The Natural Stone Institute Dimension Stone Design Manual is industry guidance for stone selection, installation, anchors, countertops, wet areas, and maintenance; access may be paid or restricted.
- A stone hatch or ST tag identifies finish intent, not hidden support, joint depth, or anchorage.

## Glass and mirror

- Determine whether the element is mirror, ordinary glazing, or safety glazing from notes, schedules, details, and location. A local leader may not classify an entire field.
- CAN/CGSB-12.1 covers safety-glazing performance, not appearance or installation. Confirm whether the governing code requires safety glazing at the specific location.
- Use current glazing-industry guidance for fabrication, edge treatment, mounting, sealants, backing, and mirror compatibility when specified. Do not infer glass type from an X operation mark.

## Metal and stainless steel

- Use the material schedule and finish designation first. Industry references such as NAAMM's Metal Finishes Manual can clarify finish terminology but do not prove alloy, gauge, substrate, or attachment.
- Preserve visible metal boundaries and interfaces. Simplifying hatch must not erase seams, returns, reveals, or adjacent-material limits.

## Paint and coatings

- MPI manuals and guide specifications are industry references for coating systems, preparation, products, and quality assurance when specified.
- Paint tags identify finish systems, not necessarily substrate or panel construction. Keep the substrate unresolved unless evidence establishes it.

## Interior finish, fire, and electrical coordination

- Building codes may regulate flame spread, smoke development, accessibility, guards, clearances, and safety glazing depending on occupancy and location. CAN/ULC-S102 is a test method referenced by Canadian codes for interior surface-burning characteristics; a drawing hatch does not prove a rating.
- The BC Electrical Code governs electrical safety for work in British Columbia. Lighting symbols and LED bands require coordination but should not be treated as electrical design approval.

When a requirement affects fabrication or installation rather than the NTS customer-confirmation view, record it as a separate review item instead of silently changing the simplified drawing.
